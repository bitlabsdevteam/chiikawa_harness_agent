01

Your first bite.

Thirty seconds to learn. Plenty of room to improve.

Take your seat

Choose Start game in the cabinet. Until then, the board is only a preview; the snake will not move and the clock is not running. You begin with a short, three-square snake, heading right across a twenty-by-twenty grid. Find the small mango before making your first turn.

Pick a direction

Use an arrow key, W, A, S or D, or the on-screen direction buttons. You do not need to hold a button to keep moving. Viper advances one square at a time in its current direction. A turn changes that direction for the next step, not halfway between squares.

Keep an exit open

Guide the head onto the mango to eat it. Each bite gives you one point and adds one square to your tail. A new mango appears in an empty square. Before chasing it, look beyond it: the safest snack is one with space to turn afterward.

A good first goal: five foods, not a perfect run.Learn how the snake responds, notice the first increase in speed, and try to finish each turn with open space ahead. A short, deliberate round teaches more than a long round spent guessing. If you need time to read, pause first; the guide will still be here.

02

Know your edges.

A simple board with very definite boundaries.

The walls are real

There are no portals or wraparound edges. Move beyond the top, bottom, left or right boundary and the round ends. The outside row is playable, but it leaves no room for an outward turn. Start turning one square before a corner rather than waiting for the head to look as though it has hit the frame.

Your body counts, too

Running into your own body also ends the round. The head may move into the square the very last tail segment is leaving on that same step, as long as you are not growing. This is a precise timing rule, not permission to cross any part of your tail. When in doubt, choose a visibly empty lane.

Nothing up our sleeve

Food does not expire, move away or grant special powers. There is only one mango at a time, and it never appears on your snake. No bonus is awarded for reaching it quickly, so you can take a longer route without losing points. Your score is simply the number of foods you have eaten in the current round.

Filling every square completes the board. With three starting segments and four hundred squares, that means a full-board score of 397. You do not need to aim for that immediately. Finding a reliable rhythm and improving your own best is the everyday challenge.

The little direction pad

On a phone or tablet, tap the four arrow buttons beneath the board. They work exactly like the keyboard and stay in the same arrangement at every screen size. Use distinct taps rather than swipes. Swiping across the canvas is not a steering gesture, and dragging there will not scroll the page.

On a keyboard, starting a round places focus on the board. Arrow keys steer instead of moving the page while you are playing. Tab still moves between controls, and Enter or Space activates the focused button. Pausing releases the arrows for ordinary page navigation. Leaving the cabinet with Tab pauses play, so reading cannot silently cost you a round.

The direction buttons do not start or resume a round. Choose the labeled Start or Resume action first. Restart clears the current round and returns you to the ready screen; it never launches a surprise new run.

04

Think in corners,
not U-turns.

One square. One decision.

No folding into yourself

If you are heading right, an immediate left command is ignored. The same applies to up followed by down. To travel in the opposite direction, make two separate right-angle turns with movement between them. You need physical space for that bend, just as you would need space to turn a long train.

Quick fingers, calm snake

Only the first valid turn between movement steps is accepted. If you tap up and then left before the snake moves, Viper accepts up and drops left. It does not secretly save a dangerous double turn for later. Wait until you see the head enter its next square, then give the second instruction.

Repeated keys and commands to continue straight do not spend your turn. An impossible reverse command does not spend it either. This keeps an accidental extra tap from changing your route, while leaving you responsible for the next real corner. At faster speeds, practice a steady tap-and-watch rhythm rather than a burst of inputs.

05

Five bites.
Another gear.

The appetite stays the same. The decisions get quicker.

The snake starts at a relaxed pace of one square every 165 milliseconds, a little over six squares per second. Every fifth food makes it faster. The change applies after that bite, so plan your exit before collecting food number five, ten, fifteen and so on. A tempting mango beside a wall can become a much tighter corner right as you eat it.

The pace window shows your current gear, not a separate score multiplier. Each mango is still worth exactly one point. Each five-food milestone adds about 0.76 squares per second. The reduction in time per square becomes smaller, even though the speed increase stays steady. Pace eight arrives at thirty-five foods, with about 88 milliseconds per square. The five notches below the scores show your progress toward the next gear. The accompanying text tells you exactly how many bites remain. After the fifth bite, the notches empty and a new five-bite cycle begins.

Step times below are rounded targets. Every five foods brings another gear, beyond those shown. If your device stalls, Viper slows or pauses rather than racing through missed moves.

06

Leave yourself
some runway.

The shortest route is not always the smartest one.

  1. Look two corners ahead. Before heading toward food, identify both your arrival lane and your departure lane. If the second lane is occupied, take a wider approach. The mango can wait; your moving head cannot.
  2. Keep big spaces connected. Cutting across the middle can divide the board into two cramped pockets. A broad route around open space usually leaves more options than a small spiral around a snack. Revisit the center when your tail has cleared it.
  3. Follow, but do not crowd. Your tail is moving away and often reveals a useful future lane. Follow at a comfortable distance rather than trying to enter its last square at the exact instant it moves. Leave yourself a correction if you misread the timing.

“First find the way out.
Then find the way in.”

No single pattern guarantees a win. Food placement changes the route, and every bite changes the shape of your available space. Use these habits as questions to ask, not a script to follow. If a route stops feeling readable, pause and trace it with your eyes.

07

A breather
is allowed.

Real life should not cost you a round.

Pause on your terms

Choose Pause, press P, or press Space while the board is focused. The snake stops in its current square. Your score, food and direction stay exactly where they are, but any turn waiting for the next step is cleared. The paused board stays fully visible so you can trace your route. The mango-colored Resume button beneath it continues the round; then steer again. You receive a full movement interval before the next square advances.

Switching tabs, minimizing the browser or leaving the game window automatically pauses a running round. Opening the guide, moving keyboard focus out of the cabinet, or scrolling the board completely out of view also pauses play. Coming back never resumes it for you. This is deliberate: you should be able to read the board and put your hand back on the controls before anything moves.

A clean second chance

After a collision, the board holds your final position and tells you whether you hit a wall or your own tail. Choose Play again for a fresh round. During a game, Restart immediately discards the current run and returns to the ready screen. It keeps your best score, but it cannot undo a mistake or restore the round you left.

A long device stall also pauses play. Short delays do not trigger a flurry of catch-up moves, so a struggling device may make the game feel slower. This cabinet favors a readable next step over punishing you for time you could not see. Reloading the page starts fresh; active rounds are not saved.

08

Room for
different players.

Clear controls. Honest expectations.

Read the state, not just the color

The cabinet names its state in text: ready, running, paused or round over. Score, best score and pace are ordinary readable text above the canvas. The snake has visible eyes, while food is a smaller outlined mango with a stem. You do not need to distinguish two similar colors to tell the head from the next snack.

All actions are real buttons with keyboard focus indicators and descriptive names. The direction pad has separate labels for up, down, left and right. You can reach the cabinet directly using the skip link at the top of the page. Browser zoom works normally; on narrow screens, the reading columns stack instead of squeezing the instructions.

What gets announced

Screen readers receive updates when you start, pause, resume, eat or finish. The board description reports head and food coordinates, measured from the top-left corner, as well as the current direction. Coordinates begin at one. It does not announce every movement step, which would overwhelm speech before a turn could be made.

Viper is still a fast, visual spatial game. These descriptions help identify state and inspect a paused board, but they are not a complete nonvisual play mode. There is no sound to rely on, no flashing celebration and no screen shake. Reduced-motion preferences remove smooth page scrolling; the grid movement remains essential to play and stops whenever you pause.

09

Your best.
Your browser.

A personal record, not a public leaderboard.

One bite at a time

Your best score updates as soon as the current score exceeds it. You do not have to finish the round to keep a new record. It is saved in this browser on this device, so returning to the same page normally brings it back. Restarting only clears the current score. It does not clear your personal best.

There is no sign-in and no online ranking. Another browser, device or private browsing session may have a different best score. Clearing site data can remove the saved record. If you open the file directly instead of visiting a hosted page, some browsers handle local storage differently; check the storage message beneath the cabinet. A higher record from another tab on the same site updates this cabinet too. Lower or malformed updates never reduce your best for this visit.

When saving is blocked or unavailable, the game still works. Your best remains available for the current visit, and the message changes to say it cannot be saved. Do not depend on that temporary record surviving a reload. There is no need to enable tracking or create an account to keep playing.

Why did my turn not happen?

You may have asked for a reverse direction, tapped before starting, or sent a second turn before the first movement step. Try one deliberate perpendicular turn, watch it land, then tap again. Remember that the direction buttons do not resume a paused game.

Why does the food seem far away?

Food is chosen from every empty square, not just nearby ones. A longer trip can be useful: it gives the tail time to clear a crowded part of the board. There is no time penalty for taking the scenic route.

What should I practice next?

Try reaching five foods without using the outermost row. Then try a round where every bite has a planned exit. Change one habit at a time and compare how much more space you have, not just the final number on the display.
