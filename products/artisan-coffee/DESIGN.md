# MARÉ / Coffee with a little coast

## Follow-up review plan
Retain the six coastal colour tokens and four original illustrations. Strengthen, rather than redesign, the existing identity: 12px minimum utility type (the small wordmark and illustration lettering excepted), roomy editorial pricing rows at tablet widths, and a paper focus ring against cobalt. Keep the desktop hero and tiled bag signature. Add a recipe choice beside the grind in the planner, recoverable clearing, and a restrained paper-only print layout. Keep the long-form sourcing copy intact.

**Critique and revision:** making every tier a giant full-width mobile card on tablet would create another generic pricing stack. Instead use two-column editorial rows at 601–800px: identity and arithmetic on the left, coffee-use guidance on the right, action below. Do not add badges, fake popularity claims or more decorative panels. Treat export clarity, navigation in short viewports, and verifiable evidence as part of the design, not polish after the fact.


## Subject-specific plan
- Identity: an original, independent Goan coffee concept, rooted in slow mornings, Indian-grown coffee and the coast. MARÉ means tide in Portuguese; use the accent consistently. Be explicit that the catalogue and subscription prices are a proposed offering, not a live shop. No invented farms, certificates, reviews, address or shipping guarantees.
- Six colour tokens: paper `#F6F3E9`, ink `#182D2B`, cobalt `#2449BE`, sea-glass `#C8DBCF`, roasted orange `#B54726`, sand `#E8DCC5`. All functional contrast must be checked; sea-glass is a background, not text.
- Type roles: Georgia for large editorial headlines and moments of warmth; system sans for navigation, copy and controls; system monospace for roast, weight and origin annotations. No external fonts.
- Wireframe: sticky masthead → two-column hero (editorial title / illustrated bag on a tiled coast) → narrow coffee principles strip → six origin cards in a 3 × 2 grid → sourcing split with illustrated hills → three subscription tiers and billing switch → four-tab brew notebook with illustrated kettle → coast-to-cup story → freshness field notes → honest service information → FAQ → take-home selection summary and footer. At 768px use two-column cards, at 360px one column, with a usable expandable navigation.
- Signature: an imperfect cobalt tile border and an orange sun behind a hand-drawn, sea-glass coffee bag; a tide-line motif reappears in rules, the bag label and section numbering. Four substantial original inline SVG scenes: coffee bag, Western Ghats, kettle/filter, and Goan window/morning table.

## First design-director critique
A generic coffee template would rely on beige, oversized empty panels, fake plantation provenance and a row of interchangeable benefit icons. The initial outline risks that, especially its principles strip and conventional pricing cards. An unqualified catalogue would also imply stock the concept cannot verify.

## Revised direction (before implementation)
Make cobalt, not beige, the organising colour: blue header wordmark, tiled hero backdrop, numbered blue section labels, cobalt subscription field. Use an asymmetric hero with a rotated bag and marginal roast notation, restrained flat cards with ruled metadata, and one dark-ink freshness section to change the reading rhythm. Keep rounded corners only for controls, never as a universal container style. Replace generic benefits with three useful buying decisions: whole bean vs ground, filter vs milk, and honest lot-level sourcing. Give the six cards region-level descriptions explicitly labelled as proposed profiles; do not manufacture estate or producer claims. A selection button must populate a useful in-page bag planner, not pretend to transact. Subscription selection must show quantities, billing total, per-month equivalent and savings, with annual costs always explicit. Use deep-linkable semantic sections, true keyboard tabs, native FAQ disclosures, a theme preference that survives reload with graceful storage failure, and mobile navigation that closes on selection and Escape. Long-form copy should teach brewing, storage and regional distinctions rather than fill space.
