# Second design-director review

Completed against the first implemented page, then resolved in the delivered source. Baseline measurements are retained in `.verification/initial-audit.json`; final browser evidence is in `.verification/results.json`. These are twelve concrete deficiencies, not future recommendations.

1. **The headline broke at the wrong rhythm.** At 1280px the first phrase wrapped, making a three-line desktop headline; at 360px the heading consumed four lines. Reduced the desktop maximum to 72px and mobile size to 48px while retaining the intentional tablet scale. **Verified:** browser geometry reports two lines at 1280 and three at both 360 and 768. See `light-1280.png` and `light-360.png`.

2. **The tablet layout discarded an original illustration.** The 800px rule hid the entire kettle figure, reducing visible SVGs to three at 768px. Replaced it with a compact two-column brew layout, retaining the single-column arrangement below 600px. **Verified:** all three target widths now expose four SVGs, containing 27, 19, 22 and 22 shape elements respectively; no horizontal overflow. The hills illustration also contains several multi-segment botanical paths.

3. **The theme control was undersized for touch.** Its original circle was 42px, falling to 40px on mobile. Set its width and height to 44px at every breakpoint without enlarging the header. **Verified:** the active-button target audit finds no sub-44px buttons in the initial state at 360, 768 or 1280px.

4. **The mobile menu's DOM order stranded keyboard users after opening.** The trigger follows the navigation, so an ordinary Tab after activation could bypass the newly visible links. Opening now focuses the first link; Escape restores the trigger; choosing a destination focuses that section; leaving the links with Tab closes the menu. **Verified:** actual CDP pointer and keyboard events exercise opening, Escape, destination focus, Tab exit and desktop resize reset successfully.

5. **Populated bag controls were harder to hit than shelf controls.** Quantity buttons were 36 × 40px, shrinking to 34px wide on mobile, and subscription removal was only 36px tall. Enlarged quantity controls to 44 × 44px, removal to at least 44px high, and permitted mobile rows to wrap rather than squeeze their labels. **Verified:** the populated 360px planner passes target-size and overflow checks; `planner-360.png` records the result. Plus/minus focus survives rerendering, including removing the final unit.

6. **Selecting a tier did not lead to its required profile choice.** A toast referred to a distant planner while leaving the user at the pricing cards. Selection now scrolls to the planner and focuses the revealed subscription-profile selector, respecting reduced-motion CSS. **Verified:** each of the three tier buttons exposes the planner, sets its pressed state and focuses `#sub-origin`; changing the profile updates the summary.

7. **Annual card prices depended on a distant explanation.** The smaller price suffix still said only “/ month”, even though the figure was an annual-prepayment equivalent. Billing updates now change each suffix to “/ month equiv.” in addition to the full upfront amount and annual saving. **Verified:** repeated monthly → annual → monthly changes check all three prices, suffixes, payments and savings; the selected subscription stays synchronised.

8. **The shortlist showed cost but not how much coffee it represented.** Six small bags could be difficult to compare mentally with a subscription quantity. Added a single-bag gram total and an explicitly approximate cup estimate at 16g per brew, separate from the subscription. Included the same information in the saved note. **Verified:** six bags show 1500g and about 93 cups; the tested download after removal shows 1250g with the correct ₹2,820 subtotal.

9. **The header count conflated bags and a subscription.** Counting a subscription as one extra “bag” misrepresented both quantity and billing. The compact indicator now says, for example, “(5 + plan)”; its accessible name spells out five single bags plus one subscription plan. **Verified:** the integration test checks both visible distinction and the accessible name after adding a tier.

10. **Without JavaScript, the page exposed inert actions and concealed recipes.** Added a clear read-only notice with mobile section shortcuts; the no-script stylesheet hides unavailable controls and exposes all four recipe panels. Moved that stylesheet into the document head. **Verified:** disabling JavaScript in Chrome exposes the notice and four recipes with zero visible buttons. The catalogue, monthly reference prices and native FAQ remain readable.

11. **The saved coffee note lost the brewing decision.** A user could choose French press and a matching grind, yet receive no recipe in the downloaded plan. The export and inline copy now include the currently selected brew method, quantities, steps and safety/tuning note; switching tabs updates the copy. **Verified:** the actual UTF-8 download includes “BREW NOTE / FRENCH PRESS” and the 30g / 480g recipe, along with the chosen grind, origin and annual payment.

12. **A blocked browser download had no readable fallback.** “Save my plan” previously relied entirely on the download mechanism. Added a native “Read or copy this plan” disclosure with selectable, wrapping plain text, shown only for nonempty plans; the save status points to it. **Verified:** its text exactly matches the downloaded file, including prices and the no-order disclaimer; clearing the plan hides the disclosure and disables empty save actions. It remains usable without clipboard permission.
